## What BranchChat is

BranchChat is a **visual branching chat app**. Instead of one linear transcript,
each conversation is a *tree* of message nodes on an infinite canvas. Users can:

- Continue from any node, or **branch** from any earlier point
- Compare branch endpoints side by side
- Tag, comment on, and link context across branches
- Replay branch history, export sessions, share read-only snapshots

The core insight we sell: **real thinking isn't linear.** Research, writing, and
problem-solving are full of forks, dead ends, and parallel hypotheses. Linear
chat throws that structure away. BranchChat keeps it.

## Who we're for

**Primary:** researchers — academics, R&D, analysts, grad students, anyone who
explores many hypotheses and needs to keep threads organized.

**Adjacent (not restricted to):** writers, founders, engineers, lawyers,
consultants, anyone doing exploratory or multi-path thinking with an LLM.

## Voice

- **Smart but not academic.** We talk to researchers without sounding like a
  journal abstract. Clear > clever.
- **Concrete.** Show the workflow, not adjectives. "Branch three prompt variants
  and compare outputs" beats "powerful and flexible."
- **Confident, not hypey.** No "revolutionary," "game-changer," "unleash."
- **Curious.** We're genuinely interested in how people think and research.
- **A little contrarian.** We have a point of view: linear chat is holding you back.

### Banned words/phrases
revolutionary, game-changer, unleash, supercharge, 10x, "in today's fast-paced
world", "the future of", excessive emojis, hustle-culture energy.

### Good vs bad

- ❌ "Supercharge your workflow with the future of AI chat! 🚀"
- ✅ "You ran six versions of that prompt and lost track of which one worked.
  Branching means you don't have to."

## Platform notes

**LinkedIn** — professional, slightly longer (max ~1300 chars before "see more").
Hook in first 2 lines. One idea per post. End with a soft question or CTA.
Researcher/founder audience.

**X / Twitter** — punchier. Single posts ≤280 chars, or short threads (3–6 posts)
for bigger ideas. Lead with the sharpest line. Lower formality, faster takes,
fine to react to news same-day.
